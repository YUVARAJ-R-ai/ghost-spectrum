CRITICAL VOLTAGE RULE: NRF24L01+ REQUIRES 3.3V POWER

Always connect the NRF24L01+ module VCC pin strictly to the ESP32 3.3V (3V3) pin. Connecting NRF24 to 5V (VIN) will instantly destroy the RF transceiver chip. The FS1000A 433MHz TX and Relay, however, require 5V for proper RF range and coil energization.

Hardware Role & Operation:

Node 1 operates concurrently on two RF domains: the internal ESP32 radio captures 802.11 Layer-2 frames (deauth, disassoc, beacons) in promiscuous mode, while the NRF24L01+ sweeps 125 discrete 1 MHz channels across 2.400–2.525 GHz to detect physical jamming carriers via the RPD register.

Capacitor Recommendation:

If you experience sporadic NRF24 resets, solder or push a 10µF to 100µF electrolytic capacitor directly across the VCC and GND pins of the NRF24 module on the breadboard to eliminate supply ripple.

Why 5V for FS1000A?

The FS1000A transmitter module works down to 3.3V, but its RF output power increases quadratically with voltage. Supplying 5V from the ESP32 VIN pin ensures maximum transmission punch to bridge 433 MHz packets through laboratory noise.

Quarter-Wavelength Antenna:

Calculate: λ = c / f = 3 × 10⁸ / 433.92 × 10⁶ ≈ 69 cm. Quarter wavelength: 69 / 4 = 17.3 cm. Solder a straight 17.3 cm copper wire to the ANT solder pad for clean resonance.

Why Digital Pin 11 for RadioHead?

On ATmega328P Arduino boards, the RadioHead RH_ASK driver defaults to Digital Pin 11 because it uses the internal 16-bit Timer 1 for jitter-free Manchester bit sampling. Do not change this pin without reconfiguring the timer prescalers.

Normally Closed (NC) Air-Gap Mechanics:

Under nominal conditions, the relay coil is unpowered; current flows freely across COM and NC. When the Arduino decodes the authenticated 8-byte 433MHz failover frame, it drives Digital Pin 7 HIGH, switching the physical telemetry channel to the 433 MHz RF modem in < 15 ms, ensuring unbroken mission-critical data transmission.

Complete Firmware Deployment & Flashing Guide Zero-Fail Bench Procedures
STEP 1: NODE 1

Flash Spectrum Sentinel (ESP32 #1)

Promiscuous Layer-2 frame sniffer & 125-channel NRF24L01+ carrier sweeper.

# Plug in ESP32 #1 via USB:
pio run -d firmware/sentinel_esp32 -t upload

Verify Serial (921,600 baud):

pio device monitor -d firmware/sentinel_esp32 -b 921600
Expected: {"node":"SENTINEL","status":"ONLINE","nrf_present":true}
STEP 2: NODE 2

Flash Mission Node (ESP32 #2)

Primary telemetry transmitter, SSD1306 OLED, & 433 MHz failover modulator.

# Plug in ESP32 #2 via USB:
pio run -d firmware/mission_node_esp32 -t upload

Verify Serial (115,200 baud):

pio device monitor -d firmware/mission_node_esp32 -b 115200
Expected OLED: LINK: 2.4GHz [OK] // OOB: 433MHz STANDBY
STEP 3: NODE 3

Flash Sub-GHz Gateway (Arduino Uno)

433 MHz RadioHead receiver & hardware channel failover relay switch.

# Plug in Arduino Uno via USB:
pio run -d firmware/airgap_node_arduino -t upload

Verify Serial (115,200 baud):

pio device monitor -d firmware/airgap_node_arduino -b 115200
Expected: [AIRGAP_NODE] RadioHead 433MHz ASK Receiver Initialized

⚡ Direct Binary Flashing via esptool.py (Under 10 Seconds)

Pre-compiled production binaries already exist in this repository. You can bypass compilation entirely and blast the pre-built binaries directly into flash memory:

# Flash Sentinel Binary Directly:

🛠️ Critical Edge-Cases & Hardware Troubleshooting

  • 1. Linux / NixOS USB Port Permission Denied:
    If you see [Errno 13] Permission denied: '/dev/ttyUSB0', grant immediate read/write access: sudo chmod a+rw /dev/ttyUSB* /dev/ttyACM*
  • 2. The ESP32 "BOOT" Button Trick:
    If upload halts at Connecting........_____....., press and hold down the physical BOOT (IO0) button on the ESP32 until the upload starts (Writing at 0x00010000...), then release.
  • 3. NRF24 Carrier Detector Floating Check:
    If Sentinel reports nrf_present: false, verify NRF24 pin 2 is on 3.3V (not 5V), check GND, and push a 10µF capacitor across the module rails.
